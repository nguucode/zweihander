# Breadcrumbs

<DocTabs>

The trail from the top of a hierarchy down to the current page, each step a
link back up.

## Variants

Breadcrumbs has one look. What changes is the size, how a long trail
collapses, and what sits between the steps.

| Form | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| `md` | Under a page title, at body size. The default. |
| `sm` | Dense headers, panels and side sheets, at `body-sm`. |
| `maxItems` | Deep hierarchies, or a trail in a narrow place: the middle collapses to "…", which expands in place. |
| `separator` | A slash or other glyph, where a chevron would clash with nearby icons. |

- **Hover** turns a link to `--foreground` and underlines it; on the
  ellipsis it fills an `--accent` chip.
- **Focus** is a 2px `--ring` outline, 2px outside the link or the
  ellipsis.
- There is no disabled state: a level the reader cannot open is left out
  of the trail.

## When to use

Use Breadcrumbs on pages three or more levels deep, under the page title,
so a reader can see where they are and jump back up any number of levels.
It is a secondary aid: the main navigation still has to get people
everywhere.

Keep the trail on one line. When it would wrap, collapse the middle with
`maxItems`; the first level and the nearest ones stay in view.

<DoDont
  do={<Story of={BreadcrumbsStories.DoCollapseLongTrail} />}
  doCaption="Collapse a long trail with maxItems: one line, and the levels that matter most stay visible."
  dont={<Story of={BreadcrumbsStories.DontWrapLongTrail} />}
  dontCaption="A trail that wraps reads as two rows and pushes the page down."
/>

Write each label as the title of the page it opens, short enough to scan.
The last item is the current page, as text: a link to the page you are on
does nothing useful.

## When not to use

- **For history.** Breadcrumbs shows where a page sits, not how the reader
  got there. For "go back", use a Back link or
  [Button](?path=/docs/components-buttons-button--docs).
- **On a flat site.** One or two levels need no trail; the main
  navigation already says where you are.
- **For the steps of a task.** Use a Stepper: it shows the steps ahead
  too, and which are done.
- **As the main navigation.** Use a Sidebar or Tabs.

## Accessibility

- A `<nav>` landmark named "Breadcrumb" (change it with `aria-label`),
  around an ordered list: screen readers announce the number of items and
  the position of each.
- Each level above the current page is a real `<a href>`, in the tab
  order.
- The last item is text with `aria-current="page"`, not a link.
- Separators are `aria-hidden`, so they are not read between items.
- The ellipsis is a button named "Show 3 more". Enter or Space expands the
  trail and moves focus to the first item it revealed, rather than
  dropping it on the page.
- The ellipsis is under 44px; on touch screens (`pointer: coarse`) its hit
  area grows to 44×44px without it growing.
- Focus ring: 2px `--ring`, 2px offset.
