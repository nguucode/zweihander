# Calendar

<DocTabs>
<DocTab value="overview">

A month grid to pick one day from, or to show one: a booking page, the
inside of a [Date Picker](?path=/docs/components-inputs-datepicker--docs).

<Status of={CalendarStories} />

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.Default} />

## Variants

Calendar has one look and one size. What changes is what it shows.

| Variant | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| One day (`value`) | Picking or showing a single day: a delivery date, a booking. |
| A span (`range`) | Showing a stay or a report period. Clicks still report one day; Date Range Picker decides the span. |
| Limits (`min`, `max`) | Booking windows: nothing before tomorrow, nothing after the season ends. |
| Ruled-out days (`isDateDisabled`) | Weekends, holidays, days already booked. |
| Locale (`locale`, `weekStartsOn`) | Month and weekday names in the reader's language, and their first day of the week. |

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.Range} />

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.MinMax} />

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.NoWeekends} />

The locale decides the week: `vi-VN` and `en-GB` start on Monday, `en-US`
on Sunday, read from `Intl.Locale` where the browser knows it (Monday
otherwise). The grid is always Gregorian, so a locale whose own calendar is
not (Persian) still gets Gregorian days, in its own digits.

## Anatomy

<Anatomy parts={CalendarStories.anatomyParts}>
  <Story of={CalendarStories.Anatomy} />
</Anatomy>

The grid always has six rows. Months span four to six weeks; a fixed height
keeps a popover or a page from jumping as you page through them.

Two more looks sit on the days: **today**, an orange disc, round where a
selected day takes the control's corners; and with `range`, the **band**, as tall as a day,
behind the days between the two ends. The band rounds off where a week or
the month starts or ends. Days of the months either side are never banded,
so two months side by side never draw the same day twice.

## Measurements

Calendar has one size. Days are 32px, 4px apart: each cell pads by half
of `--space-1` on every side, so a range's band runs unbroken under the
gap.

- **Day** has no fill at rest.
- **Today's figure is dark**, not white: white on `--orange` is 3.4:1,
  under the 4.5:1 a 14px figure needs, while `--orange-contrast` clears
  4.9:1 in both modes. A selected today shows as selected.
- **Outside days** are `--muted-foreground` mixed 50% with transparent,
  about 2:1. No token is lighter, and WCAG allows it for decoration: they
  are hidden from assistive tech and cannot be picked.
- **Unavailable days** are struck through as well as faded, so they do not
  rely on colour.

Each example forces the state on every day at once; on screen it is the
one under the pointer or the keyboard.

- **Hover** fills a day, or the previous and next buttons, with `--accent`
  behind `--accent-foreground`. The selected day and today keep their own
  colours; unavailable and outside days do not react.
- **Focus** is a 2px `--ring` outline, 2px outside the day.
- **Pressed** has no look of its own: the click selects the day, and the
  selected look follows.
- **Disabled** is per day: before `min`, after `max`, or ruled out by
  `isDateDisabled`. The previous and next buttons disable at the limits,
  at 50% opacity.
- **Selected** is the brand fill, with the control's corners; **today** is the orange disc,
  round. With `range`, both ends are selected and the days between are
  banded.

## When to use

Use a Calendar where the day is picked by looking at the month: a booking
page, a delivery slot, a few weeks either side of today. Rule out the days
that cannot be picked with `min`, `max` and `isDateDisabled`, rather than
refusing the choice after it is made.

<DoDont
  do={<Story of={CalendarStories.DoNearDate} />}
  doCaption="A date close to now, where seeing the week matters: unavailable days are struck through before anyone picks them."
  dont={<Story of={CalendarStories.DontFarDate} />}
  dontCaption="A date of birth is decades away: paging back a month, or even a year, at a time is slow. Type it in a Date Picker."
/>

Month steps keep the day where they can: 31 January plus a month is 28
February, not 3 March.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.MonthEnds} />

## When not to use

- **For a date far from today**, or one people know by heart, like a date
  of birth. Use a [Date Picker](?path=/docs/components-inputs-datepicker--docs):
  it takes the date typed, and opens this calendar when wanted.
- **For a span inside a form.** Use a
  [Date Range Picker](?path=/docs/components-inputs-daterangepicker--docs):
  it owns the two clicks that make a span and shows it here.
- **For a schedule of events.** A day here holds a number, not content.
  Use the [Event Calendar](?path=/docs/patterns-application-ui-event-calendar--docs)
  pattern, or list the events in a [Table](?path=/docs/components-data-display-table--docs).

## Accessibility

- A `role="grid"` named by the month and year ("September 2026"), with
  column headers that carry the full weekday name in `abbr`.
- Each day is a button named by its full date ("Friday 18 September
  2026"). The selected cell has `aria-selected`, and with `range` every
  cell from one end to the other does. Today has `aria-current="date"`.
- One tab stop: the selected day, or today when nothing is selected.
- Arrow keys move a day or a week, Home and End go to the week's ends,
  Page Up and Page Down move a month, and Shift+Page Up and Shift+Page
  Down a year. Enter or Space selects. Moving past the month shows the
  next one.
- Right to left, the arrows follow the screen (ArrowLeft is the next day)
  and the previous and next chevrons are mirrored.

  <Canvas of={CalendarStories.Keyboard} />

- The month and year are a polite live region, so paging is announced.
- The previous and next buttons are named "Previous month" and "Next
  month", and are disabled at `min` and `max`.
- Unavailable days stay focusable with `aria-disabled`, so they are found
  and announced as unavailable rather than skipped. Enter on one does
  nothing.
- Days of the months either side are not buttons and are hidden from
  assistive tech.
- Today's figure measures at least 4.5:1 on its disc, in both modes, on
  its own and inside a range.
