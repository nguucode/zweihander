# Resizable

<DocTabs>
<DocTab value="overview">

Two panes split by a handle that people drag, or move with the arrow keys,
to give one pane more room: a list beside its detail, a query above its
results.

<Status of={ResizableStories} />

Resizable is outside the 1.0 component list, so it starts as experimental.

<Canvas of={ResizableStories.Default} />

## Variants

| Orientation | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| `horizontal` (default) | Panes side by side: a list and the record picked from it, a file tree and the file. |
| `vertical` | Panes stacked: an editor over its output, a chart over its table. |

<Canvas of={ResizableStories.Vertical} />

`minSize` and `maxSize` keep the first pane between two sizes, in percent
of the whole, so neither pane can be dragged out of sight.

<Canvas of={ResizableStories.Limits} />

For three panes, put a Resizable in a pane of another.

<Canvas of={ResizableStories.DoNameEachHandle} />

The line is 1px; its hit area reaches past it on both sides, and grows
to 44px on touch screens without the line getting wider. The panes have no padding of their own: what goes in
them sets it. Sizes are percentages of the whole, so the split holds
when the window changes size.

Resizable fills its parent: give the parent a height for side-by-side
panes, and both a height for stacked ones.

### Differences from the spec

shadcn's Resizable is a group of any number of panels and handles
(`ResizablePanelGroup`, `ResizablePanel`, `ResizableHandle`). Zweihänder
takes flat props and two panes, `start` and `end`, as its API conventions
ask; nest one for three panes. There is no collapse on Enter and no saved
layout: store `onSizeChange` to keep the split.

## When to use

Use Resizable where people need to trade room between two things they
look at together, and the right split depends on the content: a long name
in a list, a wide table in the detail.

<DoDont
  do={<Story of={ResizableStories.DoNameEachHandle} />}
  doCaption="Each handle is named after the pane it sizes: “Resize folders”, “Resize inbox”."
  dont={<Story of={ResizableStories.DontKeepDefaultName} />}
  dontCaption="Both handles keep the default name, so a screen reader hears “Resize” twice and cannot tell them apart."
/>

Keep `minSize` above zero (the default is 10%), so a pane cannot be
dragged out of sight with its content still in the tab order.

## When not to use

- **For a fixed sidebar beside the page.** Use the App Shell template:
  its Nav Sidebar has a set width and collapses.
- **For a panel that slides over the page.** Use Drawer.
- **For a column of a table.** Use Table's `isResizable`.
- **For layouts that only change with the window.** Use the
  Multi-column layout template: Resizable is for people, not
  breakpoints.

## Accessibility

- The handle is a focusable `role="separator"`, after the APG window
  splitter. Side by side it is `aria-orientation="vertical"`; stacked it
  is `"horizontal"`.
- Its name is `handleLabel` and `aria-controls` points at the first pane.
- `aria-valuenow` is the first pane's size in percent, between
  `aria-valuemin` (`minSize`) and `aria-valuemax` (`maxSize`).
- Side by side, Arrow Right moves the handle 16px right and Arrow Left
  16px left; in right-to-left text the first pane is on the right, so
  Arrow Left grows it. Stacked, Arrow Down and Arrow Up do the same.
  Home moves it to `minSize`, End to `maxSize`. These are the keys and
  the step of Table's column resize handle.
- Dragging the handle moves it by the distance dragged, never past the
  limits.
