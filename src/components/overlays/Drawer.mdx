# Drawer

<DocTabs>
<DocTab value="overview">

A panel that slides in from an edge of the screen, over the page, for work
that sits beside what the reader was doing: filters, a quick look at a
record, a short form.

<Status of={DrawerStories} />

<Canvas of={DrawerStories.Default} />

## Variants

| Side | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| Right (default) | Details and editing: a quick look at a record from its list, a create or edit form, notifications. |
| Left | Content tied to the left of the page, such as a panel for the list or tree beside it. |
| Bottom | A short sheet as tall as its content: share, a few actions, a picker. Works best on a phone. |

<Canvas of={DrawerStories.Sides} />

`size` sets the width of a side drawer: `sm` (20rem) for filters, `md`
(28rem) for a form, `lg` (40rem) for a record with a lot to show. A side
drawer always leaves a touch target's width of backdrop, so on a phone it
is nearly full width and can still be tapped away. For short content on
a phone, pick `bottom` yourself: the side does not change with the
screen. Taller than the
screen, the body scrolls and the header and footer stay in view.

<Canvas of={DrawerStories.Long} />

On a touch screen it can also be swiped back towards its edge.

A side drawer is full height and at most 20rem (`sm`), 28rem (`md`) or
40rem (`lg`) wide. A bottom sheet is full width and as tall as its content,
up to the screen less `--target-coarse`. Borders are 1px: on the edge that
faces the page, under the header and over the footer. Backdrop and drawer
sit on the `--layer-overlay` layer and slide in over `--duration-base`.

## States

The drawer is open or closed. Opening fades the backdrop in and slides the
container in from its edge; closing reverses it. During a swipe the
container follows the finger and the backdrop fades with it. The close
button is the only part with states of its own:

  states={[

- **Hover** fills the button with `--accent`.
- **Focus** is a 2px `--ring` outline drawn inside the button.

### Differences from the spec

- **No push mode.** A drawer always covers the page with a scrim. A panel
  that pushes the page aside is part of the page's layout, not an overlay:
  build it into the page.
- **No top side.** Nothing in a business app slides down from the top
  that a Menu or a Toast does not already cover.

## When to use

Use a drawer when the reader needs the context behind it: a record looked
at from its list, filters for the table they are reading, a form that
fills in something on the page. The page stays visible, dimmed, at its
place.

<DoDont
  do={<Story of={DrawerStories.DoQuickView} />}
  doCaption="Show a record from its list in a drawer: the list stays in place behind it, where the reader left it."
  dont={<Story of={DrawerStories.DontConfirm} />}
  dontCaption="Do not ask a yes-or-no question at the edge of the screen. A decision belongs in a Modal with isAlert."
/>

Put the main action last in the footer, and dismiss actions (Cancel,
Clear) in the `secondary` variant. A form that would lose its input sets
`closeOnBackdropClick={false}`.

## When not to use

- **For a decision or a confirmation.** Use a
  [Modal](?path=/docs/components-overlays-modal--docs) with `isAlert`, or a
  [Confirm Dialog](?path=/docs/components-overlays-confirm-dialog--docs)
  before a delete: it sits in the middle, where the reader is looking.
- **For a small form tied to one button.** Use a
  [Popover](?path=/docs/components-overlays-popover--docs): it opens beside
  the button and leaves the page usable.
- **For site navigation.** Keep it in the sidebar; on a phone the sidebar
  folds to its icon rail.
- **For a long or many-step task.** Give it a page of its own.
- **Over another drawer or a modal.** Close the first, or move the second
  step into it.

## Accessibility

- `role="dialog"`, named by the title and described by the description.
- Opening moves focus to the drawer itself, so its title and description
  are read first and a phone does not pop up its keyboard; Tab then
  reaches the close button. Focus is trapped inside. The page behind is `aria-hidden` and its scroll is
  locked. Closing returns focus to the trigger.

  <Canvas of={DrawerStories.Default} />

- The body is a tab stop after the close button, so a keyboard can scroll
  a long body of text.

  <Canvas of={DrawerStories.Long} />

- `initialFocus` sends focus to another element on open, such as the
  first field of a form.

  <Canvas of={DrawerStories.InitialFocus} />

- Escape, a press outside the drawer and the close button each close it.
  The close button is named `Close` (`closeLabel`). Swiping is never the
  only way out.

  <Canvas of={DrawerStories.Dismissing} />

- With `closeOnBackdropClick={false}`, a press outside does nothing;
  Escape still closes it.

  <Canvas of={DrawerStories.KeepInput} />

- A Select opened inside a drawer lists its options above it: both sit at
  `--layer-overlay`, and the one opened last is on top.

  <Canvas of={DrawerStories.Left} />

- `side` is physical: on a right-to-left page a right drawer is still on
  the right, and swipes right to close.

  <Canvas of={DrawerStories.RightToLeft} />
