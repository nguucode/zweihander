# Modal

<DocTabs>
<DocTab value="overview">

A dialog over the page that has to be dealt with before going back: a
short form, a confirmation, a decision. Everything behind it is dimmed and
out of reach until it closes.

<Status of={ModalStories} />

<Canvas of={ModalStories.Default} />

## Variants

| Variant | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| Dialog (default) | A short task on its own: edit a profile, invite people. A close button, Escape and a press on the backdrop all close it. |
| Alert (`isAlert`) | A decision with consequences: delete, discard, leave. No close button and a press on the backdrop does nothing, so it ends only through its own buttons or Escape. |

<Canvas of={ModalStories.Alert} />

`size` sets the maximum width: `sm` (400px) for an alert, `md` (600px) for
a short form, `lg` (800px) for long content. Taller than the screen, the
body scrolls and the title and footer stay in view. On a screen under
480px wide the modal is the whole screen.

The gutter to the screen has no token: the spacing scale has no 60px
step, so the CSS adds two (`--space-12` and `--space-3`).

| Screen width | Gutter, each side | From the top | Width | Height |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 480px and up | 60px | 60px | `size`, at most the screen less 120px | Its content, at most the screen less 120px; then the body scrolls |
| Under 480px | 0 | 0 | The screen | The screen; the body scrolls, the footer sits at the bottom |

"Screen" is the box the modal opens in: the window, or the `container`
when that contains layout. Under 480px the modal has no corner radius and
no shadow.

Width is `size`: at most 400px (`sm`), 600px (`md`) or 800px (`lg`), set
in rem (25, 37.5, 50), and never wider than the screen less its gutter.
These are Atlassian's small, medium and large modals, with its 24px
padding, 12px corner and 20/24 semibold title, 16px from the title to the
body and from the body to the footer, and 8px between the footer's
buttons. The 8px from the title to the description is the kit's own:
Atlassian's header has no description. The 60px gutter, the 60px from
the top (the modal is not centred, so a body that grows does not move the
title) and the full screen under 480px are Atlassian's too. The close button is a 24px box, as
tall as the title's line. The border is 1px, and the padding counts it:
the content sits 24px from the modal's edge. Backdrop
and modal sit on the `--layer-overlay` layer and fade in over
`--duration-base`.

## States

The modal is open or closed. Opening fades the backdrop in and scales the
container up from 97%; closing reverses it. The close button is the only
part with states of its own:

- **Hover** fills the button with `--accent`.
- **Focus** is a 2px `--ring` outline drawn inside the button, so it is not
  clipped at the container's corner.

## When to use

Reach for a modal last: it stops everything else. Use one when the task
must be finished or abandoned before going on, or for a decision with
consequences. For a decision, use `isAlert`, say exactly what will happen in
the description, and name the buttons after what they do. Before a delete
or anything else that cannot be undone, use a
[Confirm Dialog](?path=/docs/components-overlays-confirm-dialog--docs): it
is this alert with the warning, the focus on Cancel and type to confirm
built in.

<DoDont
  do={<Story of={ModalStories.DoNameTheActions} />}
  doCaption="Name the buttons after what they do: Delete, Keep project. The choice reads without the question."
  dont={<Story of={ModalStories.DontOkCancel} />}
  dontCaption="OK and Cancel make the reader go back to the question to know which one deletes."
/>

Put the main action last in the footer, and dismiss actions (Cancel, Keep)
in the `secondary` variant.

## When not to use

- **For a small form tied to one button**, such as Share or a filter. Use a
  [Popover](?path=/docs/components-overlays-popover--docs): it opens beside
  the button and leaves the page usable.
- **For information**: a success, a warning, a tip. Use an
  [Inline Alert](?path=/docs/components-notifications-inlinealert--docs) or a
  [Toast](?path=/docs/components-notifications-toast--docs); neither stops
  the reader.
- **For a long or many-step task.** Give it a page of its own.
- **Over another modal.** Close the first, or move the second step into it.

## Accessibility

- `role="dialog"` (or `alertdialog`), named by the title and described by
  the description.
- Opening moves focus to the first focusable element, or `initialFocus`,
  and traps it inside. The page behind is `aria-hidden`, its pointer
  events are blocked by the backdrop, and its scroll is locked. Closing
  returns focus to the trigger.
- `isModal={false}` drops the focus trap, the scroll lock and the hidden
  page, but the backdrop still covers the page. It is for pictures of a
  modal, such as the ones on these tabs: a real modal stays modal.
- Escape closes it, as does a press outside unless `isAlert` or
  `closeOnBackdropClick={false}`. An alert dialog closes only through its
  own buttons (and Escape).

  <Canvas of={ModalStories.Dismissing} />

- **One layer with the popups.** Modal, Popover, Select and Combobox all
  sit at `--layer-overlay`. Each portals to the end of `<body>` when it
  opens, so the most recent is on top: a Select opened inside a modal
  lists its options above the modal, which a higher modal layer would
  hide.

  <Canvas of={ModalStories.WithSelect} />

- The close button is named `Close` (`closeLabel`), has the 2px focus ring
  and a 44px hit area on touch screens.
