# App Content

The page region of an app: the `main` landmark. One of the three pieces
the [App Shell](?path=/docs/templates-application-ui-app-shell--docs) template places;
the shell puts it around its children, so you rarely render it yourself.
It reads the shell's grid and canvas, so it is not used on its own.

```sh
npx shadcn@latest add https://ontheshore.biz/zweihander/r/app-content.json
```

## Flat

The Theme's `contentStyle="flat"` (the default): one card surface, inset
8px from the shell's edges at every density. Blocks inside are
[Cards](?path=/docs/components-data-display-card--docs) with
`appearance="surface"`, which become sections ruled off at the top.

<Canvas of={AppContentStories.Flat} />

## On the canvas

`contentStyle="card"`: no surface; the page sits on the canvas and each
surface Card is raised.

<Canvas of={AppContentStories.OnCanvas} />

## Props

Any `main` attribute; the App Shell passes the `id` its skip link targets.
`aside` takes a column for beside the page, in an `aside` landmark named by
`asideLabel`; the App Shell passes both through
([With an aside](?path=/docs/templates-application-ui-app-shell--docs)).

## Layout

- Below 48rem the flat surface drops its inset, edge and rounding and goes
  flush.
- The page padding is `--space-container`, `--space-section` from 64rem.
- Only the page scrolls; the shell's other pieces stay put. With an aside,
  from 80rem the page and the aside scroll on their own, side by side;
  under 80rem the aside follows the page and they scroll as one.
- The surface restates `--background` as `--card` (flat) or as the
  canvas's solid colour (card), so components that draw "the surface under
  me" match it.

## Accessibility

- The `main` landmark, focusable (`tabIndex={-1}`) so the shell's skip
  link can move focus to it.
- An aside is a `complementary` landmark after `main`, focusable so a
  keyboard can scroll it.
- On a canvas, text takes the canvas text colours (the Theme's
  `canvas-foreground` and `canvas-muted-foreground` tokens); the kit checks
  them for its own canvases.
