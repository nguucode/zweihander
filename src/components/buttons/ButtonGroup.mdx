# Button Group

<DocTabs>
<DocTab value="overview">

A selection control, like Tabs without the panel:
[Toggle Buttons](?path=/docs/components-buttons-togglebutton--docs) that
share one selection, exactly one on at a time. `type="separate"` sets them
4px apart; `type="joined"` makes them one bar.

<Status of={ButtonGroupStories} />

Button Group is outside the 1.0 component list; it is beta from 0.9.0.

<Canvas of={ButtonGroupStories.Default} />

## Variants

| `type` | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| `separate` | The default. Outlined toggles 4px apart: one view, one mode, one period at a time. |
| `joined` | A segmented control: one bar, where the options read as one control. |

## Measurements

The group adds no size of its own. Each button keeps its tier, so a group
of `sm` buttons is `--control-sm` tall.

`joined`: the inner corners are square and each toggle after the first
overlaps the one before by its 1px border, so two outlined toggles share
one line. The outer corners keep the toggles' radius.

`separate` leaves its toggles whole: every toggle keeps its four corners
and its border, and they stand `--space-1` (4px) apart in one row that does
not wrap. In a group wider than its content the toggles share the extra
width.

## Color

The group paints nothing: off and on colours are the Toggle Button's. In
a joined group the toggle that is on is raised over its neighbours so its
own border wins where they overlap.

Hover and focus are each toggle's own; a toggle has no press-and-hold
state. In a joined group the focused toggle is raised over its neighbours
so the ring is never cut; in a separate group the ring fits in the gap.

### Differences from the spec

- **A selection control, not a row of actions.** As in the Figma library,
  ButtonGroup holds ToggleButtons with exactly one on, in a `separate` or
  `joined` `type`. In 0.9 it replaced both the old action-row
  `ButtonGroup` and `ToggleGroup`. There is no `multiple`: for settings
  that combine, use separate ToggleButtons. For related actions, lay out
  Buttons yourself.

## When to use

Use a ButtonGroup when the toggles answer one question with one answer:
which view, which mode, which period. Give every toggle in a group the
same variant and appearance, so the one that is on is the only one that
stands out.

## When not to use

- **To switch between panels of content.** Use Tabs: they own the panel
  and its `tabpanel` role.
- **For a choice submitted with a form.** Use a Radio group or Checkboxes:
  a toggle applies at once, a form field waits for Submit.
- **For actions.** A ButtonGroup keeps one option on; for previous and
  next, or zoom in and out, lay out Buttons side by side.
- **For settings that combine** (status filters, Bold and Italic). Use
  separate ToggleButtons: a ButtonGroup keeps exactly one on.
- **For more than a handful of options.** Use a Select.

## Accessibility

- The container has `role="group"`; name it with `aria-label`.
- Each toggle is a native `<button>` with `aria-pressed`, so a screen reader
  announces "Board, toggle button, pressed".
- The group is one tab stop. Arrow keys move between its toggles and
  wrap, Home and End jump to the first and last; Space or Enter turns one
  on. This is the APG toolbar pattern Base UI's Toggle Group follows.
- Exactly one stays on: pressing the one that is on again does not
  release it.
- A disabled group disables every toggle in it.
- An icon-only toggle needs its own `aria-label`, as on Button. A
  [Tooltip](?path=/docs/components-overlays-tooltip--docs) can show that
  label to sighted users: it merges onto the toggle instead of wrapping
  it, so the group still holds only its toggles.
