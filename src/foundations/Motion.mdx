# Motion

Three transition durations and one spinner speed. Every `transition` in the
kit reads one of them; nothing types a number of milliseconds.

<Canvas of={MotionStories.Durations} />

## Choosing a step

The step follows what moves, not how important it is. A popup appearing
is `fast`: it answers a click, and anything slower reads as lag. A colour
or state change is `base`. Something that travels across the screen (the
tab indicator, a toast, a drawer) is `slow`, because the eye has to follow
it. If a new transition does not fit one of the three, that is a reason to
question the transition before adding a fourth step.

Easing is the browser default (`ease`) except where something grows or
shrinks: Accordion and Collapse open with `ease-out`, so the panel arrives
fast and settles. There is no easing token: two curves, each with an
obvious owner, do not need a name yet.

## Reduced motion

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the three transition steps resolve to
`0ms` in `tokens.css`, so every transition in the kit stops in one place
without each component repeating a media query.

Two things are exempt. Toast swaps its slide for a 200ms fade, written as
a number on purpose, since the tokens would turn the fade into a jump.
And spinners: a stopped spinner says nothing is happening, so instead of
stopping they slow from `--duration-spin` to `--duration-spin-reduced`.
Each spinner opts into that itself.

Loop animations with their own rhythm (the skeleton pulse, the
indeterminate progress bar) keep their own timing: they are a single
animation each, not a step anyone else should reuse.
