# Shadows

Elevation has two levels and an off switch, after Atlassian's elevation
model. Each resolves per appearance rather than being a fixed value.

<Canvas of={ShadowsStories.Scale} />

| Token | Use for | Light |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--shadow-none` | Turning elevation off | `0 0 #0000` |
| `--shadow-raised` | A surface that can be picked up and moved, such as a card being dragged | `0 1px 1px` 25% over a 1px edge at 31% |
| `--shadow-overlay` | Anything that floats above the page: menus, popovers, modals, toasts | `0 8px 12px` 15% over a 1px edge at 31% |

Each level is two layers: a soft directional layer that says how far the
surface floats, over a 1px ambient edge that keeps its outline when the
surface behind it is the same colour.

**A surface that stays put needs no shadow.** It separates from the page
by its surface and its border. Controls are flat: buttons and fields
carry borders, not shadows. Card's `elevated` appearance is the one
resting surface on `raised`, and lifts to `overlay` on hover when
clickable.

The seven step names from before (`--shadow-2xs` to `--shadow-2xl`) still
resolve: `2xs`, `xs` and `sm` read `raised`; `md`, `lg`, `xl` and `2xl`
read `overlay`. Use the two level names in new styles.

`--shadow-none` exists so that turning elevation off is a token rather
than a literal. `box-shadow: none` also works, but it cannot be swapped by
a `Theme` and it breaks a transition between two shadow states — `0 0
#0000` interpolates, `none` does not.

## Dark mode is derived, not retyped

Only the light values are written down. The dark ones are **computed from
them**: every alpha is multiplied by 4 and clamped at 0.6.

<Canvas of={ShadowsStories.Modes} />

A drop shadow works by darkening what is under it, and a dark page has
almost nothing left to darken. So the multiplier spends a small budget
well; it does not rescue dark mode.

**In dark mode, surface lightness is the primary elevation signal and the
shadow is a secondary cue.** In light mode it is the other way around. If
you are building a floating surface and it does not read as raised in dark
mode, reach for a lighter surface token, not a heavier shadow.

Deriving rather than retyping means editing a level edits both modes, and
the `Modes` story asserts the two did not collapse into the same value.

## A note on rings and shadows

`box-shadow` is a single property, so a shadow and a focus ring drawn the
same way would overwrite each other — which is why systems that draw rings
with `box-shadow` need machinery to compose the two.

The kit sidesteps it: focus is drawn with `outline`, which has its own
property and its own `outline-offset`, and current browsers follow
`border-radius` with it. An elevated surface can carry a shadow and a
focus ring at once, and neither needs to know about the other.

## What is not here

**Inset shadows.** For recessed wells and pressed states. They are a real
gap, but the kit has no recessed surface and no pressed treatment yet, so
they would be a step nothing reads. Add them as `elevation-inset-*` in `tokens/semantic.json` when
the first one lands; the alpha derivation applies unchanged.

**Coloured shadows.** A shadow tinted toward the accent instead of black.
Worth having, but it needs a real decision about which token it reads and
how it behaves in dark mode, not a mechanical port.

## Customization

The raw values are `--elevation-raised` and `--elevation-overlay` in
`:root` and `.dark`; the `*` block maps them onto `--shadow-*` and does not need to
change.

Change a level by editing `tokens/semantic.json` and running `npm run
tokens` — light and dark regenerate together. Overriding `--shadow-overlay`
directly on a `Theme` scope also works and skips the derivation, which is
the right tool for a one-off surface and the wrong one for changing the
scale.
